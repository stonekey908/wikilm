# What's Next for AI in 2026

MIT Technology Review's multi-author prediction piece for 2026 across five dimensions: Chinese AI rise, US regulation, agentic commerce, AI for science, and legal liability.

## Key Takeaways

### 1. Chinese LLMs Entering Silicon Valley Products
- [[deepseek]]'s R1 (Jan 2025) proved Chinese firms can compete with limited resources — the "DeepSeek moment" became an industry benchmark
- Open-weight Chinese models ([[qwen]] by Alibaba, Zhipu GLM, Moonshot Kimi) are increasingly chosen by US startups for their customization advantages
- Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct: 8.85 million downloads — the most downloaded open-weight model at time of writing
- The China-to-West model gap is compressing from months to weeks

### 2. US Regulatory Tug-of-War
- Trump executive order (Dec 11) aimed to preempt state AI laws
- California SB 53: requires safety testing publication + whistleblower protections
- Congress failed twice to pass a moratorium on state legislation
- [[openai]], [[meta]], and [[anthropic]] deploying super-PACs for political influence
- Federal AI law considered unlikely in 2026

### 3. Agentic Commerce
- [[salesforce]] projects AI driving $263B in online purchases in 2026 holiday season (21% of all orders)
- McKinsey estimates $3–5 trillion annually in [[agentic-commerce]] by 2030
- [[google-gemini]] Shopping Graph: can place store calls on a user's behalf
- [[openai]] launched ChatGPT shopping with Walmart, Target, Etsy partnerships
- Web traffic from search/social declining as AI chat absorbs consumer intent

### 4. LLMs for Scientific Discovery
- [[google-deepmind]]'s AlphaEvolve combined [[gemini]] with evolutionary algorithms
- Applied to data center power consumption optimization with real results
- Multiple open-source replications followed: OpenEvolve, SinkaEvolve, AlphaResearch
- Hundreds of companies investing billions in AI for drug development, math, materials science

### 5. Legal Liability Frontier
- Shift from copyright cases to chatbot liability (harm, defamation)
- Family of deceased teen suing [[openai]] (Nov 2026) over chatbot-encouraged harm
- Insurance industry potentially pulling coverage from AI companies
- Judges themselves turning to AI to handle litigation volume
- Trump executive orders complicating legal landscape
