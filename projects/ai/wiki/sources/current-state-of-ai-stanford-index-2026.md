# Current State of AI — Stanford HAI Index 2026

MIT Technology Review coverage of the [[stanford-hai]] 2026 AI Index report, authored by Yolanda Gil (USC) and others. Provides the most data-dense snapshot of AI's state available.

## Key Statistics

### Model Capability
- SWE-bench software engineering scores: ~60% (2024) → ~100% (2025)
- Models meet or exceed human expert performance on PhD-level science and math
- Robots succeed in only **12%** of household tasks — embodied AI remains hard
- [[waymo]] operating across 5 US cities; Baidu Apollo Go operating in China

### Infrastructure Scale
- AI data centers: **29.6 gigawatts** globally (≈ New York City's peak demand)
- GPT-4o annual water use may exceed drinking water needs of **1.2 million people**
- US: 5,427+ data centers; China leads in research publications and patents

### US-China Competition
- Performance gap between top US and Chinese models: razor-thin margins
- Anthropic leading as of March 2026, trailed by xAI, Google, OpenAI
- Chinese leaders: [[deepseek]], Alibaba (Qwen)
- China leads: research publications, patents, robotics deployments

### Benchmark Reliability Crisis
- Popular math benchmark contains **42% error rate**
- Models "gamed" via training data overlap
- No adequate benchmarks exist for complex agents or robots

### Labor & Employment
- Software developers aged 22–25: **~20% employment decline** since 2022
- 88% of organizations now use AI
- AI productivity gains: +14% customer service, +26% software development
- One-third of organizations expect workforce shrinkage from AI

### Public Sentiment
- 59% believe AI provides more benefits than drawbacks
- 52% report nervousness about AI
- Expert vs. public optimism gap on employment: **73% vs. 23%**
- Americans trust government **least** to regulate AI appropriately

### Regulatory Activity
- EU AI Act prohibitions on predictive policing + emotion recognition in effect
- US states passed record **150 AI-related bills** in 2025
- California SB 53: safety disclosures + whistleblower protections
- New York RAISE Act: safety protocols + incident reporting
- Federal trajectory under Trump: deregulation
