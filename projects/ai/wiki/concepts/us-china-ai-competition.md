# US-China AI Competition

The intensifying technological rivalry between US and Chinese AI labs across model capability, research output, infrastructure, and regulatory strategy.

## State of Play (Early 2026)
- **Model performance**: Near parity. Anthropic leads, trailed by xAI, Google, OpenAI; Chinese leaders ([[deepseek]], [[alibaba]] Qwen) lag by razor-thin margins
- **Research output**: China leads in AI publications and patents
- **Infrastructure**: US leads (5,427+ data centers); China leads in robotics deployments
- **Capital**: US companies have significantly more investment
- **Gap compression**: Chinese models went from months behind to weeks behind the Western frontier within 2025

## The DeepSeek Shock
[[deepseek]]'s R1 (January 2025) proved Chinese labs could match frontier performance with far less compute. This triggered the "DeepSeek moment" — a reassessment of assumptions about the resource requirements for frontier AI. US startups began routing products through Chinese open-weight models ([[alibaba]] Qwen, Zhipu GLM, Moonshot Kimi).

## Open-Weight as Geopolitical Strategy
Chinese labs open-sourcing competitive models creates a bifurcated market:
- US closed-model companies must justify cost premium
- Chinese open-weight models become infrastructure for US products — a soft influence channel
- [[allen-institute-for-ai]] (Olmo 3) competing on the open-weight side from the US

## Regulatory Dimension
- US: export controls on chips (limiting Chinese access to Nvidia); deregulatory federal posture
- China: state-sponsored research, national champions strategy
- EU: AI Act applying to both US and Chinese models used in Europe
