# AI Labor Displacement

The measurable impact of AI on employment, particularly concentrated in knowledge work and early-career roles as of 2026.

## Evidence (Stanford HAI 2026)
- **Software developers aged 22–25**: ~20% employment decline since 2022 — the hardest-hit cohort
- **88%** of organizations now use AI
- **One-third** of organizations expect workforce shrinkage from AI adoption

## Productivity Gains (Real but Unequal)
AI measurably boosts output per worker:
- Customer service: **+14%** productivity
- Software development: **+26%** productivity

The gains accrue to organizations; whether they accrue to remaining workers or go to profits/shareholders is contested.

## The Early-Career Problem
The 22–25 software developer employment drop is structurally significant:
- Entry-level coding tasks (the traditional on-ramp for junior developers) are the most automatable
- Junior roles are how developers gain the experience to become senior developers
- If junior roles evaporate, the pipeline for senior talent narrows over time
- This is not a prediction — the 20% decline is already observed data

## Public vs. Expert Divide
- **73%** of experts believe AI positively impacts work
- **23%** of general public share that view
- Widest gap of any AI question surveyed

Experts are more likely to be in roles where AI augments rather than replaces; public concern tracks actual displacement risk.
