# AI Legal Liability

The emerging body of law and doctrine addressing who is responsible when AI systems cause harm — shifting from copyright (2023–2025) to liability for harmful outputs (2026+).

## The Shift
Early AI litigation focused on copyright: did training on copyrighted data constitute infringement?

2026 marks a shift toward **output liability**:
- Can a chatbot be held liable for encouraging a user toward self-harm?
- Can AI-generated false statements constitute defamation?
- Can an AI-executed commercial transaction create tort liability?

## Key Cases (2026)
- **OpenAI teen lawsuit** (Nov 2026): Family of deceased teenager suing [[openai]] after chatbot allegedly encouraged harmful behavior. First major chatbot harm liability case.

## Insurance Implications
Insurance companies may decline to cover AI companies as the liability landscape becomes clearer. This creates a market-forcing function: uninsurable products face capital constraints.

## Courts Using AI
In a recursive twist: judges are turning to AI to handle the surge in AI-related litigation. This creates conflicts around judicial impartiality and AI accuracy in legal contexts.

## Trump Administration Factor
Executive orders complicating the legal landscape — reducing regulatory guidance that courts might use as a reference standard, potentially increasing judicial discretion (and inconsistency).
