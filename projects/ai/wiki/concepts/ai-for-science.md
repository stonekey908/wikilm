# AI for Science

The application of large language models and AI systems to accelerate or enable scientific discovery — moving beyond benchmark performance toward real-world research contributions.

## Key Development: AlphaEvolve (2025)
[[google-deepmind]] combined the Gemini LLM with evolutionary algorithms to create AlphaEvolve — a system that discovers novel algorithms autonomously. Applied to:
- **Algorithm discovery**: Found improvements to existing algorithms
- **Data center efficiency**: Optimized power consumption in real Google infrastructure

AlphaEvolve sparked rapid open-source replication:
- **OpenEvolve** (Asankhaya Sharma)
- **SinkaEvolve** (Sakana AI)
- **AlphaResearch** (independent)

## Scope of Investment (2026)
Hundreds of companies investing billions in AI for:
- Drug development and molecular design
- Mathematical proof verification and discovery
- Materials science (novel material identification)
- PhD-level science and math problem solving (models exceed human expert performance per Stanford HAI index)

## University Research
University of Colorado Denver researchers: tweaking reasoning models to improve creative problem-solving — exploring whether "creativity" can be induced via training adjustments.

## Where It's Still Hard
- Robots succeed in only 12% of household tasks — embodied AI and physical manipulation remain unsolved
- "Jagged intelligence": AI exceeds humans at PhD problems but fails at tasks a child could do
