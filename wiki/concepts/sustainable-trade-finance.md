# Sustainable Trade Finance

## Definition

Sustainable trade finance integrates environmental, social, and governance (ESG) criteria into the design and pricing of trade finance products. Rather than evaluating transactions solely on credit risk and commercial viability, sustainable trade finance incorporates a borrower's or supplier's environmental performance — creating financial incentives for greener trade and supply chain practices.

## Why It Matters to Trade Banks

Global trade supply chains are significant sources of carbon emissions and labor risk. Banks positioned as "strategic partners" (as [[global-trade-solutions]] frames HSBC's role) have both regulatory pressure and commercial opportunity to embed sustainability into their products.

[[hsbc-gts-products-and-solutions]] identifies "ESG transitions" as one of six core customer challenges HSBC's [[global-trade-solutions]] addresses — listed alongside supply chain resilience and working capital optimization as a strategic pillar, not a peripheral offering.

## Key Mechanisms

### Sustainability-Linked Trade Finance
Pricing tied to ESG performance metrics. A buyer's trade financing rate improves as their suppliers meet emissions or labor targets. Creates direct economic reward for meeting ESG milestones without restricting what the financing can be used for.

### Green Trade Finance
Financing restricted to "green" purposes: renewable energy equipment imports, low-carbon infrastructure, circular-economy goods. The use of proceeds is verified against a green framework.

### Supply Chain Scope-3 Carbon Tracking
Scope 3 emissions are a company's indirect emissions — primarily those in its supply chain (suppliers' production, logistics). Banks enabling [[supply-chain-finance]] across multi-tier supplier networks are uniquely positioned to collect and report Scope 3 data because they see transaction flows across the chain. This is noted as a knowledge gap in the wiki: specific product features for Scope 3 tracking at HSBC are not yet documented.

### Sustainable Receivables Discounting
Early payment programs where suppliers receive favorable financing rates in exchange for certifying sustainability standards (labor practices, emissions, sourcing).

## Relationship to Existing Wiki Concepts

- **[[Supply-chain-finance]]**: Sustainable trade finance often builds on supply chain finance infrastructure, adding ESG scoring layers to existing multi-tier financing programs
- **[[Trade-finance]]**: Sustainable trade finance is a specialization of [[trade-finance]], layering ESG criteria onto standard instruments (documentary credit, guarantees, receivables)
- **[[Fintech-partnerships-in-banking]]**: ESG data collection and scoring is often where fintech partnerships are most valuable — specialists in carbon tracking or supplier ESG assessment integrate into bank platforms
- **[[Regulatory-compliance]]**: ESG disclosure regulations (EU Taxonomy, TCFD, incoming ISSB standards) are driving banks to embed sustainability measurement into trade products

## Strategic Significance for HSBC GTS

HSBC's "sustainable supply chain" is one of five strategic pillars in the GTS transformation described in [[hsbc-trade-transformation-gts]]. Given HSBC facilitates $850B in annual trade across 50+ markets, systematic embedding of sustainability criteria into trade finance has the potential for outsized real-world impact on global supply chain emissions — a point not yet substantiated by specific product metrics in available sources.
