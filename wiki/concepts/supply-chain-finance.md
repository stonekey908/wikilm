# Supply Chain Finance

## Definition

Supply chain finance refers to integrated financial solutions that optimize liquidity and strengthen relationships across extended supply chains—from tier-1 suppliers through to end customers. Distinct from traditional trade finance by focusing on **ecosystem-wide capital efficiency** rather than individual transaction financing.

## Core Functions

1. **Supplier Liquidity** — Enabling suppliers to access working capital at lower cost by leveraging buyer creditworthiness
2. **Network Efficiency** — Coordinating payment terms and financing across multiple tiers of suppliers
3. **Risk Mitigation** — Reducing counterparty risk across extended supply relationships
4. **Capital Optimization** — Unlocking trapped capital in procurement cycles

## Business Model

Supply chain finance typically operates through:
- **Dynamic discounting** — Suppliers receive discounts for early payment
- **Financing integration** — Third-party (often bank) financing of supplier payments
- **Buyer-led programs** — Anchored by large, creditworthy buyers extending financing to their suppliers
- **Platform ecosystems** — Digital networks connecting buyers, suppliers, and financiers

## Distinction from Trade Finance

| Aspect | Trade Finance | Supply Chain Finance |
|--------|---|---|
| **Scope** | Individual cross-border transactions | Entire supplier network |
| **Parties** | Buyer ↔ Seller (bilateral) | Buyer → Supplier Tiers (multi-party) |
| **Duration** | Transaction-based (30-180 days) | Ongoing relationship-based |
| **Focus** | Transaction risk | Ecosystem efficiency |

## Market Applications

- **Retail & CPG** — Connecting large retailers with thousands of suppliers
- **Manufacturing** — Multi-tier component suppliers across regions
- **Automotive** — Coordinated financing across OEM and Tier-1/2 networks
- **Technology** — Component sourcing across global fabs and assembly partners
