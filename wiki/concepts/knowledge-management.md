# Knowledge Management

The practice of capturing, organizing, and applying knowledge—either individual or organizational—to improve decision-making, preserve expertise, and augment cognitive capacity.

## Historical Context

- **[[zettelkasten]]** — Niklas Luhmann's handwritten card system (1951–1997), demonstrated that *linked knowledge* compounds value
- **[[llm-wiki-pattern]]** — Modern digital variant: LLM-maintained structured knowledge bases
- **[[second-brain]]** — Contemporary metaphor for externalized, searchable personal knowledge systems

## Modern Approaches

| Approach | Method | Strength |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** | Chunk-and-search | Fast, stateless |
| **LLM Wiki** | Structured markdown linked pages | Composable, human-readable |
| **Personal Language Models** | Continuous retraining on user data | Adaptive, deeply personalized |

## Core Challenges

1. **Information overload** — capturing without drowning in inputs
2. **Organization decay** — structures that made sense become outdated
3. **Retrieval friction** — knowledge you can't find is knowledge you can't use
4. **Integration with action** — knowing vs. doing

## Value Proposition

Effective knowledge management enables:
- **Decision augmentation** — decisions informed by your accumulated experience
- **Knowledge preservation** — expertise survives the person; insights survive reorganizations
- **Cognitive enhancement** — offloading memory to systems that can search, link, and synthesize
- **Organizational learning** — preventing repeated mistakes, accelerating onboarding

## Technologies & Systems

- **[[obsidian]]** — markdown note app with local graph visualization
- **[[llm-wiki-pattern]]** — using LLMs to maintain structured knowledge bases
- **[[personal-language-models]]** — AI models trained on individual knowledge
- **[[agentic-ai]]** — autonomous systems that reason over knowledge bases to take action
