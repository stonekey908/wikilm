# Second Brain

## Definition

A "second brain" is an externalized knowledge system that augments human memory and reasoning by capturing, organizing, and connecting information outside the biological brain. The concept spans handwritten card systems, digital note-taking apps, and AI-maintained wikis, but shares a common purpose: reducing cognitive load while compounding knowledge over time.

## Historical Forms

- **[[zettelkasten]]** — [[niklas-luhmann]]'s physical card index system; the foundational second-brain archetype. Connections between cards created emergent insights the author never planned.
- **Personal Knowledge Management (PKM)** — Digital successors (Roam, [[obsidian]], Notion) that replicate Zettelkasten structure in software.
- **[[llm-wiki-pattern]]** — The current frontier: an LLM maintains the wiki from raw sources, synthesizing and cross-linking automatically rather than relying on human curation.

## The LLM-Era Second Brain

[[andrej-karpathy]] popularized using LLMs as the maintenance layer: raw sources go in, structured interlinked markdown comes out. This inverts the traditional PKM model — instead of the human organizing notes, the LLM compiles and connects them.

Key properties of the LLM-wiki second brain:
- **Compilation metaphor** — raw research is "compiled" into organized [[knowledge-compilation|knowledge]] just as source code is compiled into executable programs
- **Knowledge graphs** — wikilinks create a graph of concepts that surfaces connections the human wouldn't notice manually
- **Persistent synthesis** — every ingest enriches the same persistent base; insights compound rather than accumulate as isolated notes

## Relationship to Personal Digital Twins

[[personal-language-models]] extend the second brain concept further: rather than just organizing knowledge, they model the individual's reasoning style, communication patterns, and preferences — enabling AI that thinks *like* the user, not just *for* the user.
