## Core terms

- **[[llm-wiki-pattern]]** — System where an LLM reads raw sources and maintains a structured, interlinked wiki; no human edits the wiki layer. Contrasted with RAG.
- **[[retrieval-augmented-generation]]** — Chunk-and-embed approach: AI re-discovers knowledge from scratch at query time; no persistent structure or memory accumulates.
- **[[knowledge-compilation]]** — Metaphor for the ingest step: raw research "compiled" into organized, queryable wiki pages, akin to source code → binary.
- **[[digital-twin]]** — Live virtual replica of a physical asset, process, or person, synced to real-time sensor data for simulation and optimization.
- **[[agentic-ai]]** — Autonomous AI that reasons, plans, executes multi-step actions, and adapts toward defined outcomes with minimal human intervention.
- **[[world-models]]** — Neural networks learning to simulate physical dynamics (physics, object permanence, spatial coherence), enabling prediction before acting.
- **[[predictive-maintenance]]** — Data-driven maintenance strategy (top digital-twin ROI driver): act on failure predictions, not fixed schedules.
- **[[supply-chain-finance]]** — Liquidity optimization across multi-tier supplier *networks* — distinct from bilateral trade finance (buyer ↔ single seller).

## What NOT to confuse

- **RAG ≠ LLM wiki pattern.** RAG chunks documents into embeddings and searches at query time — nothing accumulates. The wiki pattern compiles sources into persistent, human-readable pages once; queries read structured knowledge, not raw chunks. [[karpathy-second-brain-medium-article]]
- **Supply-chain finance ≠ trade finance.** Trade finance covers bilateral guarantees and letters of credit (buyer ↔ seller). Supply-chain finance optimizes liquidity across multi-tier *networks* of suppliers — a fundamentally different scope. [[hsbc-gts-products-and-solutions]]
- **Advisory AI ≠ autonomous AI.** Advisory agents surface recommendations; humans approve actions. Autonomous agents make binding decisions without pre-approval. Deloitte and banking regulators treat these as categorically different risk levels. [[deloitte-agentic-ai-banking]]
