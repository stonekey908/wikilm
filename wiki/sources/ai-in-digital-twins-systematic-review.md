# AI in Digital Twins: A Systematic Literature Review

## Overview

Comprehensive systematic literature review analyzing 149 peer-reviewed studies examining the intersection of artificial intelligence and digital twin technologies across multiple application domains and industries.

## Key Findings

### Critical Integration Gaps

The review reveals a significant disconnect between academic interest and practical implementation:

- **Virtual-to-Physical Integration Deficit**: The majority of publications do NOT demonstrate actual virtual-to-physical connections
- **Limited Real-Time Data Flow**: Few studies establish genuine, continuous data synchronization between physical systems and virtual models
- **AI-Component Bias**: Most publications focus on AI implementation and testing rather than comprehensive [[digital-twin]] modeling and integration

### What's Missing

The field demonstrates strong potential, but practitioners often:
- Treat AI as a component to integrate rather than designing holistic systems
- Lack bidirectional data flows (physical → virtual → physical feedback)
- Fail to move beyond proof-of-concept AI testing

## Methodology

**Type**: Systematic literature review
**Scope**: 149 peer-reviewed studies
**Approach**: Pattern analysis, gap identification, best-practice extraction

## Critical Takeaway

> Achieving truly functional digital twins requires moving beyond AI component testing toward holistic systems that maintain continuous, **bidirectional data synchronization** between virtual models and physical assets.

This finding directly challenges the "AI-first" mentality common in early-stage digital twin projects. Success requires systems thinking: architecture, connectivity, and data governance before AI optimization.

## Implications for Implementation

1. **Bidirectional data flow is non-negotiable** — not optional for production twins
2. **System architecture comes before AI optimization** — getting data flows right matters more than AI sophistication
3. **Real-time synchronization challenges deserve more research attention** than they currently receive in academic literature
